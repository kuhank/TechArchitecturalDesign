FIELD OF THE INVENTION

[1] The present invention relates to the control of autonomous decision-making systems. More particularly, it relates to a system and method that measures, separately for each of a plurality of output dimensions, how strongly a small perturbation of the input is amplified in the output of an autonomous agent; that applies a corrective intervention only to those dimensions in which the amplification exceeds a threshold; that preserves controlled variability in dimensions designated as exploratory; and that schedules re-validation of multi-step agents according to an estimated error-growth rate. The autonomous agent may be a software agent driven by a generative model, a language-model-based database query generator, a planner of an autonomous robot or vehicle, or a monitoring system for physiological, behavioural or aggregate social time series.

BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

[2] Autonomous agents built on large generative models produce an output by iterating a nonlinear map: each generated element is fed back as input for the next one, and in multi-step operation the results of earlier steps and of external tools are appended to the input. Such agents exhibit sensitive dependence on initial conditions. A small, meaning-preserving change in the input, such as a reworded instruction, a reordered list or a formatting change, or a single erroneous element early in the output, can lead to a substantially different final output. In multi-step operation such deviations grow and compound, so that the probability of completing a long task correctly falls sharply with task length.

[3] In the theory of nonlinear dynamical systems, the rate at which nearby trajectories separate is quantified by the Lyapunov exponent, and an initial error e₀ grows to a tolerance Δ after a predictability horizon T_(h) = λ⁻¹ln (Δ/e₀). Further, it is known from chaos-control methods (for example the Ott–Grebogi–Yorke method) that a chaotic system can be stabilised by small perturbations applied only when its trajectory leaves a target region.

[4] Existing techniques for improving the reliability of generative agents have several limitations:

1.  Uncertainty-estimation techniques, such as entropy over sampled answers or self-consistency checks, compute a single scalar score for the whole output. They do not identify which part of a structured output is unreliable.

2.  Majority voting over complete outputs requires entire outputs to coincide. The probability that a sample is fully correct is the product of the per-part correctness probabilities and falls rapidly as the number of parts grows.

3.  Verification of every step or every part of an output, and uniformly low sampling temperatures, suppress all variability equally. This increases computational cost (the number of model invocations), and it removes useful variability from parts of the output where diversity is desirable, such as alternative plans, explanations, or exploratory behaviour of a robot.

4.  Re-validation of multi-step agents is performed at fixed intervals that do not adapt to how quickly a particular task diverges. Fast-diverging tasks are checked too rarely and slow ones too often.

5.  In autonomous robots, exploratory or creative behaviour and safety constraints are typically handled by a single global setting, so that increasing exploration also increases the risk of unsafe actions.

[5] There is therefore a need for a system that localises instability to specific output dimensions, intervenes minimally and only where instability is harmful, deliberately retains controlled variability where it is beneficial, and schedules re-validation according to measured error growth.

OBJECTS OF THE INVENTION

[6] The principal object of the invention is to provide a system and method for selective chaos control of an autonomous agent, in which instability is measured and controlled separately for each of a plurality of output dimensions.

[7] Another object is to provide a dimension-wise divergence measure and a dimension-wise finite-step exponent computed from an ensemble of perturbed inputs. Together these form a “chaos map” of an output.

[8] Another object is to estimate, without labelled data, an error-rate range for each output dimension from its observed divergence.

[9] Another object is to provide a minimal-intervention controller for each dimension that selects the lowest-cost corrective action from an ordered intervention ladder only when the divergence of that dimension leaves a target band, thereby reducing the number of model invocations compared with verifying all dimensions.

[10] Another object is to provide per-dimension variability control (“selective chaos”), in which precision dimensions are driven to low divergence while exploratory dimensions are held within a divergence band. The controller gain is limited below a computed critical value to prevent oscillation of the controller.

[11] Another object is to provide an “agent Lyapunov time” and a predictability-horizon-based scheduler that triggers re-validation of a multi-step agent before its deviation reaches a tolerance.

[12] A further object is to apply the above to autonomous robots, so that creative or exploratory decision dimensions remain varied while safety dimensions are held stable and unsafe actions are vetoed or corrected before actuation.

SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

[13] According to one aspect, the invention provides a system comprising at least one processor and a memory, the processor being configured to:

1.  receive an input describing a task or situation;

2.  generate a plurality K of perturbed versions of the input, each preserving the meaning of the input, and determine a perturbation magnitude ε;

3.  obtain from an agent a candidate output for each perturbed input;

4.  decompose each candidate output into D output dimensions by a decomposition module, each dimension being associated with a distance measure and with a role selected from at least a precision role and an exploratory (creative) role;

5.  compute, for each dimension j, a divergence d_(j) as the mean pairwise distance between the candidate values of that dimension, and a finite-step exponent λ_(j) = ln (max(d_(j),d_(min))/ε);

6.  for each precision dimension whose divergence lies within its target band, accept a consensus value of that dimension;

7.  for each precision dimension whose divergence lies outside its target band, select and apply the lowest-cost intervention, from an ordered set comprising consensus, grounded regeneration of that dimension only, external verification and escalation, whose predicted post-intervention error does not exceed a tolerance;

8.  for each exploratory dimension, adjust a dimension-specific variability parameter so that its divergence remains within a predetermined band;

9.  compose a final output or action from the selected values of all dimensions after checking constraints between dimensions; and

10. for multi-step operation, estimate an error-growth rate from a series of observed deviations and schedule the next re-validation at a fraction of the predictability horizon.

[14] According to another aspect, the invention provides a corresponding computer-implemented method, and an autonomous robot comprising sensors, actuators and the above system, wherein dimensions relating to safety (such as speed, force, separation from persons and rule compliance) are assigned the precision role and dimensions relating to route, strategy or approach are assigned the exploratory role, and wherein an action is passed to the actuators only after the master controller composes it from admissible dimension values.

[15] The technical effects of the invention include: a reduction in the number of model invocations needed to reach a given output accuracy; localisation of errors to specific output dimensions; prevention of unsafe actuator commands while retaining exploratory behaviour; stable (non-oscillating) operation of the variability controller; and an increased success rate of multi-step agents for a given number of re-validation operations.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

[21] The following description sets out embodiments by way of example. The terms “agent”, “model” and “planner” are used broadly to denote any component that maps an input to an output, including a generative language model, a code or query generator, a robot motion planner and a forecasting model. Variations apparent to persons skilled in the art fall within the scope of the invention.

A. System overview (FIG. 1)

[22] Referring to FIG. 1, the system comprises an input interface (102) that receives an input describing a task or a situation, such as a natural-language request, a database schema, sensor readings, a map or a goal. A perturbation module (104) produces K variants of the input, K preferably being between 3 and 15 and most preferably 5. A generator (106) produces a candidate output for each variant. A decomposition module (108) maps each candidate output to a tuple of D dimension values. A chaos sensor (110) computes a divergence and an exponent for each dimension. A bank of dimension controllers (112), one per dimension, applies interventions selected from an intervention ladder (114). A master controller (116) checks consistency between dimensions and composes the final output (120). A horizon manager (118) schedules re-anchoring for multi-step operation.

B. Perturbation module (104)

[23] The perturbations are chosen to preserve meaning. Examples include synonym substitution, paraphrasing, reordering of independent clauses, addition or removal of politeness or framing phrases, changes of formatting, and independent sampling of the generator with a non-zero sampling temperature. For a robot, they include small perturbations of sensor estimates within their noise bounds and alternative random seeds of a sampling-based planner. The perturbation magnitude ε is computed as the mean distance between the original input and its variants, for example one minus the token-set Jaccard similarity, or the distance between sentence embeddings. Where the variants are independent samples of the same input, ε may be set to a constant reference value, for example 0.15.

C. Decomposition module (108)

[24] The decomposition module splits a candidate output into semantically distinct dimensions, each with a canonical representation and a distance ρ_(j) ∈ [0,1].

[25] In a database-query embodiment, the output is a structured query that is parsed into a syntax tree and split into six dimensions: (1) referenced tables; (2) projected columns or expressions; (3) join clauses; (4) filter predicates, with top-level conjunctions split so that the order of predicates does not matter; (5) grouping and aggregate functions; and (6) ordering and limit clauses. Each dimension is canonicalised (for example normalised to lower case, with identifiers resolved), and ρ_(j) is the Jaccard distance between the sets of canonical elements. An explanatory text accompanying the query is a seventh dimension with the exploratory role.

[26] In a robot embodiment, the dimensions include the selected route or waypoint sequence, the manipulation strategy, the velocity profile, the applied force, the minimum separation from persons and obstacles, and compliance with operating rules. In a plan-generating embodiment, the dimensions include the ordered list of steps, the tools invoked, the parameters of each tool call and the final answer. In a code-generating embodiment, they include function signatures, called interfaces, control-flow structure and literal values.

[27] Each dimension is assigned a role. A precision dimension is one in which deviation from the correct value is harmful. An exploratory (creative) dimension is one in which variation is acceptable or beneficial. Each dimension further has a weight w_(j) and a target divergence band [θ_(j)^(lo),θ_(j)^(hi)]. For precision dimensions the band is preferably [0,θ_(j)] with θ_(j) between 0.2 and 0.8; for exploratory dimensions it is preferably [0.2,0.6].

D. Chaos sensor (110)

[28] For each dimension j the chaos sensor computes the divergence as the mean pairwise distance over all pairs of the K candidate values:
d_j = (1 / C(K,2)) × Σ over all pairs a<b of ρ_j( y_j(a), y_j(b) ),
and the finite-step exponent
λ_j = ln( max(d_j, d_min) / ε ),
where d_(min) is a resolution floor, for example 10⁻³. A positive λ_(j) indicates that dimension j amplifies input perturbations (unstable or “chaotic”); a negative λ_(j) indicates contraction (stable). The vector of exponents for all dimensions forms the chaos map of the input. Each dimension is classified as stable, borderline or chaotic by comparing λ_(j) with two thresholds.

[29] Error-rate range from divergence. For a dimension compared by exact match, in which a sample is correct with probability 1 − p and otherwise takes wrong value i with probability q_(i), the expected divergence is 1 − (1−p)² − ∑_(i)q_(i)². It follows that
1 − √(1 − d_j)  ≤  p  ≤  ½ (1 − √(1 − 2 d_j)),
the lower bound corresponding to diffuse wrong values and the upper bound to a single wrong alternative. The sensor reports this range as an estimated error rate for each dimension without requiring labelled data, and the controllers use it to predict the error of the consensus action.

[30] Multi-step operation. In a multi-step agent the sensor records d_(j)(t), or a deviation e(t) from a validated reference, at each step. It estimates a growth rate λ as the least-squares slope of ln e(t) over a recent window, for example the last eight steps.

E. Dimension controllers and intervention ladder (112, 114)

[31] Each precision dimension has a controller that remains inactive while the divergence of the dimension is within its band; in this case the consensus (most frequent, or medoid) value of the K candidates is accepted at no additional cost. When the divergence leaves the band, the controller selects, from the ordered ladder below, the lowest-cost action whose predicted post-intervention error does not exceed a tolerance p_(j)^(tol):

1.  consensus value (no additional invocation), used when the agreement share is at least a threshold, for example 0.6;

2.  grounded regeneration of that dimension only: the generator is invoked once more at low or zero temperature, with additional context specific to the dimension, such as schema facts, retrieved records, sensor ground truth or a dimension-focused instruction, and only the value of that dimension is taken from the result;

3.  external verification, such as executing a probe query on the database, running a unit test, checking a physics or kinematic model, or checking against a rule base;

4.  escalation to a human operator, or, for a robot, a safe stop.

This threshold-triggered, least-cost intervention corresponds to the principle of chaos control, in which small perturbations are applied only when the trajectory leaves a target region. The expected number of invocations per input is K plus the number of dimensions requiring grounded regeneration, compared with one invocation plus one per dimension when every dimension is verified.

[32] Factorised consensus. Because consensus is taken separately for each dimension, a dimension’s correct value is recovered whenever its own error rate is below one half. A complete output need not coincide across samples. For independent dimensions, the probability that factorised majority voting gives a wrong output is at most Σ_j exp(−2K(½ − p_j)²), whereas whole-output voting requires 1 − Π_j(1 − p_j) < ½, a strictly stronger condition.

F. Selective chaos: per-dimension variability control

[33] Each dimension has its own variability parameter, for example a sampling temperature τ_(j). In a robot it may be an exploration noise level or a sampling radius. The parameter is updated by the log-proportional feedback law
u_(n + 1) = u_(n) − η (d_(j)−d_(j)^(⋆)),   u = ln τ_(j),
where d_(j)^(⋆) is the midpoint of the dimension’s band. Precision dimensions are thereby driven toward low temperature and low divergence, while exploratory dimensions are held inside their band. Dimension-specific parameters are realised by generating dimensions in separate invocations, by constrained or structured decoding with field-specific sampling settings, or by separate sub-planners.

[34] Gain limit. If g_(j)(τ) denotes the expected divergence of dimension j at parameter τ and τ^(⋆) satisfies g_(j)(τ^(⋆)) = d_(j)^(⋆), the feedback law is stable if and only if 0 < η < η_(c) = 2/(τ^(⋆)g_(j)′(τ^(⋆))). At η = η_(c) the controller undergoes a period-doubling bifurcation and above it oscillates between near-zero and high variability (FIG. 3). The system estimates τ^(⋆)g_(j)′(τ^(⋆)) from recent pairs of (parameter, divergence) and keeps η below a fraction, for example 0.5, of η_(c).

G. Horizon manager and agent Lyapunov time (118)

[35] For multi-step operation, the reciprocal 1/λ of the estimated growth rate is the agent Lyapunov time, and T_(h) = λ⁻¹ln (Δ/e₀) is the predictability horizon, where e₀ is the deviation immediately after a re-validation and Δ the tolerance. The horizon manager triggers re-validation (re-anchoring), such as verification of the current state, grounded regeneration, or a reset to the last validated state, every max (1,⌊αT_(h)⌋) steps, with 0 < α < 1, preferably 0.5 to 0.8. This bounds the worst-case deviation by e₀(Δ/e₀)^(α) < Δ under a deterministic growth bound (FIG. 5). The growth rate is preferably obtained from earlier runs of tasks of the same type, stored per task type. Where it must be estimated during the task from the readings revealed at re-validations, the horizon manager uses a conservative estimate, namely the pooled rate over all readings weighted by interval length plus at least one standard error, because under-estimating a fast rate causes failures. Where the deviation can be monitored continuously, re-validation is instead triggered when the monitored deviation exceeds a fraction of the tolerance. When several agents or tasks share a budget of re-validations, intervals proportional to 1/λ_(i) minimise the total number of re-validations for a common deviation target.

H. Master controller (116)

[36] The master controller collects the selected value of each dimension, checks constraints between dimensions, and sends any dimension involved in a violation back through the intervention ladder. Examples of constraints are that projected columns must belong to referenced tables, and that a planned route must respect the speed and separation limits. It then composes the final output or action and computes a global stability index
S = 1 − (Σ_j w_j d_j) / (Σ_j w_j), with S between 0 and 1,
which is reported together with the chaos map and the per-dimension error-rate ranges. Outputs whose stability index falls below a threshold may be withheld or flagged.

I. Autonomous robot embodiment (FIG. 4)

[37] Referring to FIG. 4, sensors (402) provide the situation. A planner (404) produces K candidate actions or plans under perturbation. Exploratory dimensions (406), such as route, strategy and approach, retain controlled variability, which produces diverse and novel candidate behaviours. Safety dimensions (408), such as speed, force, separation from persons and rule compliance, are assigned the precision role; their controllers suppress divergence and veto or project any candidate whose safety dimensions leave the band or violate a limit. The master controller (410) composes an admissible action, which alone is passed to the actuators (412). The horizon manager schedules re-planning according to the estimated growth rate of the deviation between predicted and observed states. The robot thereby explores creatively without permitting unsafe actions.

J. Further embodiments

[38] Monitoring of physiological and cognitive signals. The chaos sensor is applied to multichannel signals (for example EEG channels or wearable sensor channels), each channel or derived feature being a dimension. Per-dimension exponents and horizons indicate which components are stable and which are irregular, and threshold crossings trigger alerts.

[39] Human activity and mobility tracking. Per-dimension exponents over activity features determine which activity components are predictable and over what horizon, and schedule recalibration of the tracking model.

[40] Aggregate pattern analytics. For aggregate, area-level event series (for example time of day, location cell, event type and season), the system reports the predictability and horizon of each dimension separately rather than a single forecast. The minimal-intervention principle limits over-confident long-range use. This embodiment is directed at aggregate patterns and not at profiling individuals.

[41] Multi-agent systems. Each agent of a group is treated as a set of dimensions, and divergence between agents is controlled in the same manner, preventing amplification of small perturbations across the group.

K. Example

[42] In a simulated evaluation of a six-dimension structured-output agent over 4000 inputs, with K = 5, ties broken at random, and grounded regeneration triggered when d_(j) > 0.7:

-   The system reached a full-output accuracy of 92.1% with 5.57 model invocations per input.

-   Whole-output majority voting reached 54.3% with 5 invocations.

-   Verifying every dimension reached 85.7% with 7 invocations.

-   The dimension-wise divergence identified wrong dimensions with an area under the ROC curve of 0.968.

The advantage over verifying every dimension is largest when grounded regeneration is imperfect.

[43] In a simulation of 3000 multi-step agents with heterogeneous growth rates, with the deviation revealed only at re-validations, horizon-based re-validation using known growth rates gave a 97.5% success rate against 80.8% for fixed-interval re-validation at the same mean number of re-validations. With rates estimated during the task, the conservative estimator improved on the fixed interval at moderate budgets. In the gain sweep of the variability controller, oscillation began within 0.2% of the computed critical gain.

L. Implementation

[44] The system may be implemented on a general-purpose computer, a server, an embedded controller of a robot or vehicle, or distributed across a client and a remote model service, with the modules described above stored as instructions in non-transitory memory and executed by one or more processors. The generator may be local or accessed through a network interface. For the robot embodiment, the system is connected between the planner and the actuator interface.
